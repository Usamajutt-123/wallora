If your phone or monitor has an OLED panel, fully black areas behave differently from illuminated colors. Individual black pixels can switch off, creating deep contrast and potentially reducing the display power used by the wallpaper; total device impact varies with brightness, screen-on time and interface content.

## Why pure black wallpapers behave differently

LCD panels generally keep a backlight running behind dark pixels, while OLED and AMOLED pixels emit their own light. A fully black OLED pixel can turn off, so a black-heavy static screen can use less display power than a bright one under comparable settings. That does not translate into a fixed battery-life gain for every device.

## Four styles that suit AMOLED screens

1. **True-black minimalism** — a single glowing line or shape on pure black. It keeps the composition simple and can make bright icons easier to distinguish.
2. **Neon accents on black** — thin neon glows (purple, lime, cyan) create depth without lighting up half the panel.
3. **Dark cosmic scenes** — deep space shots are naturally black-heavy. A nebula hanging in true darkness is about as good as AMOLED gets.
4. **Subtle gradients** — a slow fade from black into deep navy reads as premium, and hides smudges better than flat black.

## FAQ

### Do AMOLED wallpapers really save battery?

Fully black pixels can switch off on OLED and AMOLED panels, so a black-heavy static screen can use less display power than a bright one under comparable settings. The overall battery difference varies and may be small relative to apps, radios and screen brightness.

### What resolution should I pick for a phone?

Match the image to your phone's native dimensions and aspect ratio. A larger source can provide useful cropping room, but source quality and compression matter as much as the pixel count.
